Methods & data

How each signal was collected, built and tested, for both countries, and what the data cannot say. Every number on the country pages is computed from the raw files in the repository by the scripts alongside them.

01

Design

Four research questions are asked of each country, using the same window (January 2016 to October 2026, monthly) and, where possible, the same sources.

Discourse signals (what people, investors and governments say): search interest, encyclopaedia reading, online headlines and their sentiment, equity prices of listed space firms, parliamentary or congressional activity, and regulatory documents.

Hard indicators (what is actually built and funded): orbital launch attempts, satellites launched by owner type, licensed operations, and private capital as reported or filed.

Each question tests whether a discourse signal moves with, ahead of, or independently of the hard indicators. A signal that only reacts to a few spectacular events is useful for sentiment but not as a proxy for demand.

03

Methods

Standard, transparent techniques chosen so that each result can be re-run from the files.

Attention indices

Each component is log-transformed (log 1 + x), standardised to a z-score over its own history, and averaged across components available that month. India: Public Attention Index from Google Trends and Wikipedia for ISRO and state missions; NewSpace index from private-firm and IN-SPACe terms. US: Public Attention Index from NASA, Artemis and Space Force searches, Wikipedia reading of those three articles, and Hacker News stories; NewSpace Attention Index from five company and market searches, Wikipedia reading of five company articles, and Hacker News stories about them. Results without Wikipedia are reported as a robustness check.

Regressions and lead–lag

Monthly OLS of each index on launch attempts, failures and event dummies, with Newey–West (HAC, 3 lags) standard errors. Lead–lag uses Granger tests at lags 1–3 on stationary series (differenced attention, excess returns; ADF-checked) and cross-correlations at ±6 months. Annual relationships use Spearman rank correlation.

Event studies

Daily abnormal return = basket return minus benchmark return (market-adjusted model). Cumulative abnormal return over trading days [0,+1] and [0,+5]. The t-statistic divides by the standard deviation of daily abnormal returns over days −120 to −11, scaled by the square root of window length. Events that happen after the market close start on the next trading day. Basket members enter only after their listing date.

Sentiment

Headline sentiment is scored with VADER (Hutto & Gilbert, 2014), a lexicon model returning a compound score from −1 to +1. Event shifts compare the mean in the 7 days from an event with days −60 to −8 (Welch t-test). Trend is an OLS of monthly mean on time.

Policy coding

Parliamentary questions and bill titles are coded by transparent keyword rules (published in the analysis code), not by hand. Era comparisons use Fisher's exact test.

04

Verification

Steps taken to keep every figure tied to its source.

Every series pulled through a browser was checksummed (sum and index-weighted sum) in the browser and again after transcription, and the two had to match. Federal Register and Congress totals were checked against the sources' own facet counts. De-SPAC and IPO dates for each US basket member were checked against company or exchange announcements. Event dates were checked against at least one primary or news source. Every number quoted in the text of the country pages is generated from the analysis output rather than typed by hand, and a script compares the page text with those outputs before publication.

05

Limitations

What these results cannot support.

Both countries

Not causal. Lead–lag and event tests show timing, not cause. Multiple testing: each page runs dozens of tests; isolated results near p = 0.05 should be read as leads, not findings. Google Trends values are sampled and rescaled for each pull, so exact values shift slightly between downloads. X (Twitter) and Reddit were not collected: X's API is paid; Hacker News is used as a substitute, which skews towards an English-language tech audience. VADER misses domain language: it scores "rocket explodes" as neutral.

Country-specific

India: NewSpace firms are unlisted, so the equity basket measures established aerospace and defence suppliers. Private-capital figures come from secondary reporting and disagree with one another. US: the NewSpace basket has survivorship bias (Astra, Virgin Orbit, Terran Orbital and Maxar are missing from the price source). The January 2025 Starship window overlaps the presidential inauguration. Wikipedia views are counted on current article titles only, so reading of a page under an earlier title is not included (Starship before November 2018, Artemis before May 2019). Congressional indexing of the 119th Congress is incomplete. FAA operation counts were taken from one FAA forecast document.

Reproduce

Repository layout.

data/ raw India files · data_us/ raw US files · analysis/ India scripts · analysis_us/ US scripts. Each raw file begins with a header giving its source, query and pull date. Python 3 with pandas, numpy, scipy and statsmodels reproduces every statistic.
