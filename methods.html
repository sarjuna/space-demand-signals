Methods & data

How each signal was collected, built and tested, for both countries, and what the data cannot say. Every number on the country pages is computed from the raw files in the repository by the scripts alongside them.

01

Design

Four research questions are asked of each country, using the same window (January 2016 to October 2026, monthly) and, where possible, the same sources.

Discourse signals (what people, investors and governments say): search interest, encyclopaedia reading, online headlines and their sentiment, equity prices of listed space firms, parliamentary or congressional activity, and regulatory documents.

Hard indicators (what is actually built and funded): orbital launch attempts, satellites launched by owner type, licensed operations, and private capital as reported or filed.

Each question tests whether a discourse signal moves with, ahead of, or independently of the hard indicators. A signal that only reacts to a few spectacular events is useful for sentiment but not as a proxy for demand.

03

Methods

Standard, transparent techniques chosen so that each result can be re-run from the files.

Attention indices

Each component is log-transformed (log 1 + x), standardised to a z-score over its own history, and averaged across components available that month. India: Public Attention Index from Google Trends and Wikipedia for ISRO and state missions; NewSpace index from private-firm and IN-SPACe terms. US: Public Attention Index from NASA, Artemis and Space Force searches, Wikipedia reading of those three articles, and Hacker News stories; NewSpace Attention Index from five company and market searches, Wikipedia reading of five company articles, and Hacker News stories about them. Results without Wikipedia are reported as a robustness check.

Regressions and lead–lag

Monthly OLS of each index on launch attempts, failures and event dummies, with Newey–West (HAC, 3 lags) standard errors. Lead–lag uses Granger tests at lags 1–3 on stationary series (differenced attention, excess returns; ADF-checked) and cross-correlations at ±6 months. Annual relationships use Spearman rank correlation.

Event studies

Daily abnormal return = basket return minus benchmark return (market-adjusted model). Cumulative abnormal return over trading days [0,+1] and [0,+5]. The t-statistic divides by the standard deviation of daily abnormal returns over days −120 to −11, scaled by the square root of window length. Events that happen after the market close start on the next trading day. Basket members enter only after their listing date.

Sentiment

Headline sentiment is scored with VADER (Hutto & Gilbert, 2014), a lexicon model returning a compound score from −1 to +1. Event shifts compare the mean in the 7 days from an event with days −60 to −8 (Welch t-test). Trend is an OLS of monthly mean on time.

Policy coding

Parliamentary questions and bill titles are coded by transparent keyword rules (published in the analysis code), not by hand. Era comparisons use Fisher's exact test.

IDX

The Space Signals Index

A quarterly comparison of attention with build-out, built only from series already on the country pages.

Construction

Attention combines the Google Trends and Wikipedia components of the country attention indices: for India, the public (ISRO, Chandrayaan, Gaganyaan, PSLV, Department of Space) and NewSpace (Skyroot, Agnikul, Pixxel, IN-SPACe, NSIL, "space startup") terms; for the US, the public (NASA, Artemis, Space Force) and NewSpace (SpaceX, Starlink, Blue Origin, Rocket Lab, Starship, "space stocks") terms, plus Hacker News story counts. Each component is log(1 + x), averaged over the trailing 12 months.

Build-out combines orbital launch attempts and commercial satellites launched (GCAT), each as log(1 + trailing 12-month count).

Each component is standardised against its own January 2016 to December 2025 baseline. A sub-index is the mean of the components available that month, re-standardised against the same baseline and scaled so that 100 is the baseline average and 10 points is one standard deviation. Gap = Attention − Build-out. A quarter takes the value at its last month, and only complete quarters are reported.

Because the baseline is fixed, adding new months does not change earlier values (apart from provisional months, below). Values before 2026 are scaled with the full 2016–2025 averages, so for out-of-sample forecasting rebuild them with expanding-window standardisation from the component CSVs.

NewSpace terms are included from 2016; before the firms existed their search interest is near zero, which is itself part of the signal. India’s commercial satellite series is thin (17 satellites, January 2016 to July 2026), so India’s build-out moves in steps.

Robustness and limits

Component choice. Correlation of attention with a Google-only version: India r = 0.79, US r = 0.92; with a Wikipedia-only version: India r = 0.51, US r = 0.69. Dropping any single component moves attention by at most 8.7 points (India) and 4.7 points (US). The dashboard chart shows this range as a band.

Provisional months. The GCAT satellite catalogue is complete only to July 2026, so the satellite component is held at its July 2026 value for August to October 2026 and will be revised once the catalogue catches up. Launches are complete to October.

Interpretation. Each country is measured against its own history, so the index compares directions, not levels, across countries. Equal weights are a transparent default, not an estimate. Gaps within ±5 points (half a standard deviation) are treated as "in line", and "clearly ahead" means a gap beyond ±5. The index describes; it has not yet been shown to predict prices, investment or launches. Testing that, with vector autoregression and Granger tests, is the next step.

Files. India quarterly, India monthly, US quarterly, US monthly. Code: code/index/build_index.py.

VAR

Time-series tests (vector autoregression)

Do attention, prices and launches lead one another? Four two-variable VARs, each tested five ways.

Models

Attention and share prices. India: monthly change in NewSpace attention (CAI) with the listed suppliers’ excess return over the Nifty 50, October 2020 to October 2026 (73 months). US: change in NewSpace attention (NAI) with the NewSpace basket’s excess return over the S&P 500, September 2021 (when the basket reaches six firms) to October 2026 (62 months).

Attention and launches. India: change in public attention (PAI) with orbital launches per month; US: change in NAI with the monthly change in log(1 + launches). Both February 2016 to October 2026 (129 months).

Stationarity. Attention levels trend (US NAI: ADF p = 0.62), so attention enters as monthly changes; every series in every model passes an ADF test (p < 0.01). Returns are log excess returns in per cent.

Event controls. Event months enter as exogenous dummies: flagship-mission months in all models, plus Starship flight months and the SpaceX listing month in the US price model. Dummies with no event in a sample are dropped.

Estimation. Lag length chosen by AIC (maximum 6 months). Every model is stable, and the main (AIC) models show no residual autocorrelation up to 12 lags (Portmanteau p ≥ 0.16).

Tests and robustness

Granger F-tests in both directions. A lead is called robust only if p < 0.05 in all five versions: lags by AIC, lags by BIC, a fixed three lags, no event dummies, and a sample ending May 2026 (before the SpaceX listing and Vikram-1). Eight main tests are run, so a Bonferroni threshold is 0.05 ÷ 8 = 0.0063.

Impulse responses are orthogonalised and cumulative over 12 months, for a one-standard-deviation surprise, with 95% asymptotic bands. The surprised series is ordered second, so it cannot move the other series in the same month and the response shows leads only.

Limits. Granger causality is about timing, not cause. Samples are short, so modest effects can be missed. Monthly data cannot capture reactions within days, which the event studies on the country pages measure. The two-variable models leave out other drivers, such as overall market sentiment beyond the benchmark.

Files. Granger results, all versions, impulse responses. Code: code/var/run_var.py.

04

Verification

Steps taken to keep every figure tied to its source.

Every series pulled through a browser was checksummed (sum and index-weighted sum) in the browser and again after transcription, and the two had to match. Federal Register and Congress totals were checked against the sources' own facet counts. De-SPAC and IPO dates for each US basket member were checked against company or exchange announcements. Event dates were checked against at least one primary or news source. Every number quoted in the text of the country pages is generated from the analysis output rather than typed by hand, and a script compares the page text with those outputs before publication.

05

Limitations

What these results cannot support.

Both countries

Not causal. Lead–lag and event tests show timing, not cause. Multiple testing: each page runs dozens of tests; isolated results near p = 0.05 should be read as leads, not findings. Google Trends values are sampled and rescaled for each pull, so exact values shift slightly between downloads. X (Twitter) and Reddit were not collected: X's API is paid; Hacker News is used as a substitute, which skews towards an English-language tech audience. VADER misses domain language: it scores "rocket explodes" as neutral.

Country-specific

India: NewSpace firms are unlisted, so the equity basket measures established aerospace and defence suppliers. Private-capital figures come from secondary reporting and disagree with one another. US: the NewSpace basket has survivorship bias (Astra, Virgin Orbit, Terran Orbital and Maxar are missing from the price source). The January 2025 Starship window overlaps the presidential inauguration. Wikipedia views are counted on current article titles only, so reading of a page under an earlier title is not included (Starship before November 2018, Artemis before May 2019). Congressional indexing of the 119th Congress is incomplete. FAA operation counts were taken from one FAA forecast document.
