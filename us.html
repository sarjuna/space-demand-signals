United States

The same test as the India page, run on the world's largest space economy: do public attention, online sentiment, investor prices and federal policy activity track the real build-out in launches, satellites and licensed operations? Every series below is pulled from a public source and traced to it.

Data as of 6 Nov 2026 Window Jan 2016 – Oct 2026 (monthly) Discourse: Google Trends (US) · Wikipedia · Hacker News · NYSE/Nasdaq prices · Congress · Federal Register · SEC EDGAR Capacity: GCAT launch & satellite catalogue · FAA licensed operations
RQ1

Does public attention track launch cadence?

Public Attention Index (PAI): mean z-score of log Google Trends (US) for NASA, Artemis and Space Force, English-Wikipedia reading of the NASA, Artemis program and US Space Force articles (per million total views), and the monthly count of Hacker News stories about NASA, Artemis or the Space Force. NewSpace Attention Index (NAI): Google Trends for SpaceX, Starlink, Blue Origin, Rocket Lab and "space stocks", Wikipedia reading of SpaceX, Starlink, Blue Origin, Rocket Lab and Starship, and Hacker News stories about SpaceX, Starship, Starlink, Blue Origin or Rocket Lab.

RQ2

Do investors price space events, and does attention lead prices?

Unlike India, the US has listed NewSpace companies. NewSpace basket: equal-weight portfolio of ten space-native firms, each entering after its de-SPAC or IPO date (Virgin Galactic, AST SpaceMobile, Spire, Rocket Lab, Redwire, BlackSky, Planet, Intuitive Machines, Voyager, Firefly). SpaceX (SPCX, listed 12 June 2026) is kept out of the basket. Benchmark: S&P 500. Also shown: the Procure Space ETF (UFO), a broader, partly non-US basket.

Cumulative return above the S&P 500

Compounded monthly return relative to the S&P 500 (−50% means half the S&P's performance). Basket from Sep 2021 (when it reached six members); UFO from May 2019.

Lead–lag: NewSpace attention vs basket

Correlation of monthly ΔNAI at t with basket excess return at t+k, Sep 2021–Oct 2026. Positive k: attention leads; negative k: prices lead. Filled bars: p < 0.05 (uncorrected).

RQ3

Does policy activity lead or follow the build-out?

Two federal records. Congress: all 415 bills and resolutions indexed by the Library of Congress under "Space flight and exploration" or "Spacecraft and satellites", 113th–119th Congresses (2013–2026), classified by title. Federal Register: all 1,072 documents from 2014 to October 2026 whose text mentions launch vehicles, spacecraft, orbital debris, outer space, space launch or commercial space.

Commercial share of space legislation

Share of space-indexed bills and resolutions whose title concerns commercial or private activity, by Congress. 119th Congress still being indexed.

Per year, by issuing agency. FAA licenses launches; FCC licenses satellites and spectrum. 2026 runs to October.

RQ4

What does online sentiment say, and does it move with events?

NLP layer: 17,640 Hacker News story titles (2016–2026) mentioning US space programmes or companies, each scored with VADER, a standard lexicon-based sentiment model (compound score −1 to +1). Hacker News stands in for X and Reddit, which could not be collected here; it is an English-language, tech-industry audience.

Story volume

Hacker News stories per month mentioning state programmes (NASA, Artemis, Space Force) or NewSpace firms.

Title sentiment

Monthly mean VADER compound score (thin) and 12-month rolling mean (thick).

Sentiment around triggers

Mean title sentiment in the 7 days from the event minus the mean over days −60 to −8, with 95% interval (Welch). Filled: p < 0.05.
