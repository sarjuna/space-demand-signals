RQ1

Does public attention track launch cadence?

Public Attention Index (PAI): mean z-score of log Google Trends (India) for ISRO, Chandrayaan and Gaganyaan, and English-Wikipedia views of ISRO, PSLV and Department of Space per million total views. NewSpace Attention Index (NAI): the same construction over Skyroot, AgniKul, IN-SPACe, "space startup", Pixxel and NSIL, from Sep 2020.

RQ2

Do investors react to space events, and does attention lead prices?

Investor proxy: equal-weight basket of seven NSE-listed aerospace and defence suppliers (MTAR, Paras, Astra Microwave, Centum, Data Patterns, Apollo Micro, Azad Engineering), measured as return in excess of the Nifty 50. The first five have documented ISRO/space business; Apollo Micro and Azad are mainly defence and aerospace. Robustness: using only the five space-exposed firms, the Chandrayaan-3 result strengthens (+8.5%, t = 4.4) and the Space Policy 2023 result weakens to t = 1.9. Defence news is a confound throughout.

What could and could not be measured

The brief listed X, Reddit, SEC filings and Google Trends. India-specific equivalents were substituted where the original sources were unavailable or not India-relevant.

Method notes

Indices. Each component is log(1+x), z-scored over its own available months, then averaged; a month needs ≥4 PAI or ≥5 NAI components. Wikipedia views are divided by total English-Wikipedia user views to remove platform-wide trends. ISRO's article moved title in Jun 2023; both titles are summed.

Attention regression. OLS of monthly PAI on orbital launches, failures and a flagship-mission dummy (Chandrayaan-2 Jul–Sep 2019; Chandrayaan-3, Aditya-L1 and Gaganyaan TV-D1 Jul–Oct 2023), HAC standard errors (3 lags).

Event study. Market-adjusted abnormal returns (basket minus Nifty 50, log), t = CAR / (σAR·√L) with σ from days −120 to −11. t0 is the first trading day on or after the event.

Lead–lag. Granger F-tests (lags 1–3) on stationary series (ADF p < 0.01), Sep 2020–Oct 2026 for NAI (73 months) and Feb 2016–Oct 2026 for PAI (128 months). A permutation test (2,000 shuffles) checks the one significant PAI lag.

Parliament topics. Rule-based keyword classifier on question titles, first match wins in the order NewSpace → policy → sustainability/security → international → exploration → launch/infrastructure → applications → budget/workforce. Titles were read once to tune rules; a few remain borderline (for example "Space Programmes for Socio Economic Development" is counted as NewSpace).
